I-CIP remains firm amid contrasting Arabica
and Robusta trends
Green coffee prices:
The ICO Composite Indicator Price (I-CIP) averaged 261.26 US cents/lb in October 2026, down 0.3% from
September 2026.
• The I-CIP decline masked divergent movements across the market, with average Arabica prices increasing while
Robusta prices declined. The prices of the Brazilian Naturals, Colombian Milds and Other Milds increased by 1.5%,
0.5% and 1.7% in October 2026 compared with September 2026, averaging 294.49, 357.85 and 334.99 US cents/lb,
respectively. In the same month, the Robustas declined 2.0% to 164.19 US cents/lb.
• The arbitrage between the London and New York futures markets widened by 4.9% to 132.54 US cents/lb in
October 2026.
Exports of coffee:
In September 2026, global green bean exports totalled 10.19 million bags, up 5.8% as compared with
9.63 million bags in September 2025. Two of the four coffee groups recorded declines, with the following
dynamics in September 2026:
• Green bean exports of the Robustas increased by 15.7% to 4.07 million bags.
• Exports of the Colombian Milds increased by 12.6% to 1.19 million bags.
• Green bean exports of the Brazilian Naturals decreased by 2.8% to 3.32 million bags.
• Shipments of the Other Milds decreased by 1.7% to 1.61 million bags.
• Total green bean exports of the Arabicas increased to 6.13 million bags in September 2026, up 0.2% from
6.12 million bags in September 2025.
Global exports of all forms of coffee decreased by 0.7% to 11.55 million bags in September 2026 as compared
with 11.63 million bags in September 2025. Two of the four regions recorded falls, with the following dynamics
in September 2026:
• Exports of all forms of coffee from Asia & Oceania were down 3.9% to 3.65 million bags.
• Exports of all forms of coffee from Africa increased by 2.8% to 1.53 million bags.
• Exports of all forms of coffee from South America increased by 2.6% to 5.84 million bags.
• Exports of all forms of coffee from the Caribbean, Central America & Mexico decreased by 19.3% to
0.53 million bags.
• The decline in global soluble exports in September 2026 versus September 2025 was the main factor behind the
drop in global exports of all forms of coffee.
The share of soluble coffee in all forms of coffee fell to 12.5% in coffee year 2025/26, from 12.8% in
coffee year 2024/25.

Exports by coffee group – green beans
In September 2026, global green bean exports totalled 10.19 million bags, up 5.8% as compared with
9.63 million bags in September 2025. This marked the fourth consecutive month of positive growth in
green bean exports, with a total of six months of expansion during coffee year 2025/26, following two
consecutive months of decline at the start of coffee year 2025/26. As a result, green bean exports reached
123.93 million bags in coffee year 2025/26, up 1.3% from 122.33 million bags in the previous coffee year.
The Robustas were the main driver of export growth in September and over the coffee year as a whole,
pushing their share of total green bean exports to 40.4% in coffee year 2025/26. This marks the first time
on record that the Robustas’ share has exceeded the 40% threshold.
A more in-depth export analysis for coffee year 2025/26 will be presented in the next issue of the Coffee
Market Report.

Exports by region – all forms of coffee
Global exports of all forms of coffee decreased by 0.7% to 11.55 million bags in September 2026 as
compared with 11.63 million bags in September 2025. This marked the fifth month of negative export
growth in a coffee year characterized by fluctuating monthly dynamics, which ultimately resulted in exports
of all forms of coffee increasing by 1.0% to 142.39 million bags in coffee year 2025/26, up from
141.02 million bags in coffee year 2024/25.
In September, declines in Asia & Oceania and the Caribbean, Central America & Mexico outweighed
increases from Africa and South America. Over the coffee year as a whole, however, Asia & Oceania was the
main region driving the growth in global exports of all forms of coffee, recording a net gain of 5.81 million
bags, which more than offset the combined net losses from Africa and South America.

Explanatory Note for Table 3
For each year, the Secretariat uses statistics received from Members to provide estimates and
forecasts for annual production, consumption, trade and stocks. As noted in paragraph 100 of
document ICC-120-16, these statistics can be supplemented and complemented by data from
other sources when information received from Members is incomplete, delayed or inconsistent.
The Secretariat also considers multiple sources for generating supply and demand balance sheets
for non-Members.
The Secretariat uses the concept of the marketing year, that is the coffee year commencing on
1 October of each year, when looking at the global supply and demand balance. Coffee-producing
countries are located in different regions around the world, with various crop years, i.e. the 12-
month period from one harvest to the next. The crop years currently used by the Secretariat
commence on 1 April, 1 July and 1 October. To maintain consistency, the Secretariat converts
production data from a crop year basis to a marketing year basis depending on the harvest
months for each country. Using a coffee year basis for the global coffee supply and demand, as
well as prices, ensures that analysis of the market situation occurs within the same time period.
For example, the 2023/24 coffee year began on 1 October 2023 and ended 30 September 2024.
However, for producers with crop years commencing on 1 April, the crop year production occurs
across two coffee years. Brazil’s 2023/24 crop year began on 1 April 2023 and finished 31 March
2024, covering the first half of coffee year 2023/24 (October 2023–March 2024). However,
Brazil’s 2024/25 crop year commenced 1 April 2024 and ended on 31 March 2025, covering the
latter half of coffee year 2023/24 (April–September 2024). To estimate production for coffee year
2023/24, the Secretariat therefore allocates a portion of the April–March 2023/24 crop year
production and a portion of the April–March 2024/25 production into 2023/24 coffee year
production.
It should be noted that while estimates for coffee year production are created for each individual
country, these are made for the purpose of creating a consistent aggregated supply-demand
balance for analytical purposes and do not represent the production occurring on the ground
within the individual countries.
